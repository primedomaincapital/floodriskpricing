What flood insurance actually costs

Published National Flood Insurance Program (NFIP) premium averages, coverage limits, and claims figures under FEMA's Risk Rating 2.0 pricing.

Standard homeowners policies do not cover flood damage, so flood cover is bought separately through the NFIP or a private carrier. Under Risk Rating 2.0, introduced in 2021, NFIP premiums are set per property — distance to water, elevation, rebuilding cost, flood frequency, and flood type — rather than from flood zone alone. That is why two homes in the same zone can pay very different premiums, and why a single national average is only a starting point. The rows below separate national averages, zone averages, and state averages, and state figures from different sources and dates are never averaged together.

How to read these numbers

Included: national and zone NFIP averages from 2026 NFIP data as summarized by BeSecuredNow from FEMA materials and NerdWallet analysis, OpenFEMA-derived Texas and Nebraska averages with their stated windows, and program rules (limits, waiting period, increase cap, Community Rating System discounts). Excluded: private-carrier quotes, which are property-specific and not published as averages, and any blended national average across sources — the $976 figure and the $915 snapshot figure come from different windows and methods and are shown side by side with that warning, never combined. Averages describe policies in force, not the price any one homeowner will be quoted; elevation, rebuilding cost, and distance to water move individual premiums sharply.
